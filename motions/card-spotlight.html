Card Spotlight

Two soft lights follow the cursor: a wash on the card surface and a glow along the border. The surface one is easy. The border one is the detail that separates a polished component from a first-attempt grid — and it needs a mask, not an overflow: hidden.

The prompt

Add a pointer-tracked spotlight to a grid of cards — a soft wash across the surface plus a glow along the border.

Surface highlight:
- On pointermove, compute the pointer position relative to the card's bounding box and write it into two CSS custom properties on that card: --mx and --my, in pixels.
- Render the wash with a ::before pseudo-element covering the card: radial-gradient(200px circle at var(--mx) var(--my), rgba(120,160,255,.16), transparent 65%).
- Fade it in with opacity on :hover, not by toggling display — display changes cause layout work.

Border glow:
- Use a ::after pseudo-element with inset: 0, border-radius: inherit and padding: 1px.
- Give it the same radial gradient but brighter, then punch out the middle with a mask:
  -webkit-mask: linear-gradient(#000,#000) content-box, linear-gradient(#000,#000);
  -webkit-mask-composite: xor;
  mask-composite: exclude;
- Do not try to do this with overflow: hidden — that clips the glow to the inside edge and it disappears.

Constraints:
- Cache the bounding rect on pointerenter; never call getBoundingClientRect() inside pointermove.
- Write only custom properties. Never set left/top on the highlight element.
- Skip the whole effect under @media (hover: none) so touch devices are unaffected.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion: keep the static hover state, remove the transition timing.
- Keep a visible :focus-visible outline — the spotlight is decorative and must never be the only indication of focus.

What actually makes it work

  • Pixels, not percentages. A gradient positioned with percentages changes its centre as the card resizes and drifts oddly near the edges. Writing the pointer position in pixels against the card's own box keeps the highlight exactly under the cursor.
  • The border glow needs a mask, not clipping. A radial gradient on ::after fills the whole box. padding: 1px plus mask-composite: exclude keeps only that 1px ring, which is why the glow appears to travel along the edge. overflow: hidden would cut it off at the inner edge and hide the effect entirely.
  • Cache the rect. getBoundingClientRect() forces layout. Calling it on every pointermove (which can fire at 120Hz+) makes the card feel heavy. Compute it once on pointerenter.
  • Two style writes per frame. Setting --mx and --my on the card invalidates only paint for that card's pseudo-elements. No layout, no sibling recalculation.

Compatibility and performance

AspectDetail
CSS custom propertiesUniversal.
mask-compositeChrome 120+, Safari 15.4+, Firefox 53+ via the standard property. In older Safari only the -webkit- form applies — ship both.
DegradationIf masking is unsupported the border glow is simply absent; the surface wash still works and the card is unchanged.

The one thing to watch is the number of cards. Twenty spotlit cards on one page means twenty pseudo-element pairs repainting as the pointer moves. That is still cheap, but on a low-end device it is the first thing to drop — in which case keep the effect on the hovered card only and remove it from the rest.

When not to use it

Don't use it to imply interactivity a card doesn't have. A light that follows the cursor reads as "this is clickable". If the card is not a link, add no cursor change and no hover lift — or better, remove the effect.

Don't stack it with a tilt and a glow and a scale. Three simultaneous pointer reactions on one card is the most common way this effect goes from premium to gimmicky.

Don't ship it without a keyboard story. Pointer-only feedback means keyboard users see nothing. Pair it with a clear :focus-visible outline of equal prominence.
