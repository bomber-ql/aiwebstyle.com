Claymorphism vs Illustration

Both are soft, both are warm, and both avoid the straight line. One gets there with shadow; the other gets there with geometry.

Short answer: Claymorphism rounds everything to a pill or a 26px card and lifts it with a paired shadow. Illustration keeps the corners but makes every one of them different — a radius like 240px 20px 220px 18px, so each block reads as a cut-out rather than a component.

What actually separates them

Clay is a shadow style. Cards sit at 26px radius, buttons are 999px pills, and depth comes from two outer shadows — one cool grey offset down-right, one pure white offset up-left. Hovering lifts the card 2px and grows both. Nothing is insett; everything rests on the surface.

Illustration is a geometry style. The radii are deliberately unequal per corner, blocks carry a 2px outline in a soft tint (2px solid #a9d8bf, or #f0dcc0), and the whole page tilts by a single degree. There is no drop shadow at all — the hand-drawn impression comes from asymmetry, not from light.

Decision table

A filled dot marks the side that handles the dimension more convincingly. It is a judgement, not a rule — several rows are genuinely a tie.

Dimension Claymorphism Illustration Focused
Corner treatment 26px cards, 999px pills — uniform ● Unequal per corner, e.g. 240px / 20px / 220px / 18px
Depth ● Two outer shadows, light and dark None — no drop shadow anywhere
Outline None; shadow defines the edge ● 2px solid in a soft tint
Tilt None ● rotate(-1deg) on the brand mark
Palette Blue, mint, pink, cream pastels Warm sand, sage, terracotta
Motion ● Hover lifts 2px, shadow grows None; the drawing is static

When each one wins

Claymorphism wins when

  • The interface has real controls and the softness should not remove the sense of a button being pressable
  • The audience is consumer or education, where pastel and lift read as friendly rather than childish
  • You need a system — one radius, two shadows — that a team can apply consistently

Illustration Focused wins when

  • The page is a story or a small brand site with few components and a lot of personality
  • You want warmth without pastel, which the sand-and-sage palette gives you
  • Every block is unique anyway, so a per-corner radius costs nothing

Steering a model to one or the other

Both prompts should say "no straight lines", which is why models produce similar output. The split is shadow: ask for a paired outer shadow with a 6px offset and you get clay; say explicitly that there is no shadow at all and the softness comes from unequal corner radii plus a 2px tinted outline, and you get illustration. A model given "soft rounded cards" will add a drop shadow by default and land on clay every time.
