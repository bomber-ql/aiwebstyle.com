Abstract Geometric vs Neo-Swiss

Both are compositions made of circles, squares and type on a grid. One uses shape as the layout; the other uses columns and never draws a shape at all.

Maintained by AI Web Style·Updated 28 October 2026

Short answer: Abstract geometric places a large circle and a rotated square as the structure itself, then fits the type against them. Neo-Swiss has no shapes: a 12-column grid, a headline across nine columns, a standfirst starting at column seven, and one red used twice.

What actually separates them

The abstract page has geometry you can see. A circle at 50% radius, a square rotated 18 degrees with a 28px radius, pill buttons at 999px, cards at 24px with a 1px #ece6dd hairline. The headline is capped at 16ch because it has to sit in the gap the shapes leave, not because it needs a measure.

Neo-Swiss has geometry you can only infer. Everything is placed on repeat(12, 1fr): the headline takes 1 / span 9, the standfirst 7 / span 5, so the two blocks overlap across three columns. Corners are 0 everywhere, there are no shadows, and red appears exactly twice on the page.

Decision table

A filled dot marks the side that handles the dimension more convincingly. It is a judgement, not a rule — several rows are genuinely a tie.

Dimension Abstract / Geometric Shapes Neo-Swiss
What you see ● Circles, rotated squares, pill buttons Columns and rules only
Layout device Shapes define space; text fits around them 12-column grid with deliberate overlap
Corner ● 28px square, 24px cards, 999px pills 0 everywhere
Accent Pastel fills at low opacity, blending where they overlap One red, used twice
Typeface Montserrat, heavy Helvetica Neue, one family
Headline measure 16ch grid-column: 1 / span 9

The CSS that changes

These are the declarations that make the difference visible. Everything else — font sizes, spacing, section order — is shared, because both pages use the same markup.

Property Abstract / Geometric Shapes Neo-Swiss
Structure shapes as blocks, text fitted against them grid-template-columns: repeat(12, 1fr)
Headline max-width: 16ch grid-column: 1 / span 9
Standfirst max-width: 50ch grid-column: 7 / span 5
Decoration border-radius: 50% / rotate(18deg) border-radius: 0; no shapes

When each one wins

Abstract / Geometric Shapes wins when

  • The page has few words and needs a composition to hold the eye
  • You want a modern, designed feel without photography
  • The brand is creative and can carry a pastel palette

Neo-Swiss wins when

  • The page carries several kinds of content and needs a system rather than a composition
  • The brand is institutional — finance, engineering, academia — where restraint reads as competence
  • You want the layout itself to look designed while staying entirely in black and white plus one red

Steering a model to one or the other

The instruction that separates them is whether shapes are allowed. For abstract geometric, name the shapes and their sizes — one circle at 50%, one 28px square rotated 18 degrees — and say the text is fitted against them. For neo-Swiss, state the column spans for the headline and the standfirst explicitly and forbid decorative shapes entirely. A model asked for "geometric layout" will add circles to a Swiss grid, which produces neither: columns that justify nothing, decorated with shapes that align to nothing.
