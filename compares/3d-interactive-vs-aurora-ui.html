3D Interactive vs Aurora UI

Both are dark, both are blue, both move. The difference is whether the depth is in the objects or in the air behind them.

Short answer: 3D interactive puts depth on the elements: stacked text-shadow on the headline, cards that lift 3px on hover and tilt toward the pointer. Aurora puts depth in the background: three blurred radial fields drifting on 14 to 22 second cycles behind panels that barely move.

Both panels load the same page — only the stylesheet differs.

What actually separates them

The 3D page earns its name from two things — a headline built from a multi-line text-shadow stack that imitates extrusion, and cards at 10px radius with a 1px #7f9bff border and a 0 10px 24px -10px blue shadow. Interaction is the point: hover translates the card up 3px, and the tilt follows the pointer.

Aurora is a background technique. Three circles at 50% radius, each a radial gradient fading to transparent at 66%, animated with small translate and scale keyframes so they drift out of sync. The panels in front use backdrop-filter: blur(14px) and a 1px rgba(255,255,255,.18) border — they are glass, and they are deliberately static.

Decision table

A filled dot marks the side that handles the dimension more convincingly. It is a judgement, not a rule — several rows are genuinely a tie.

Dimension 3D Elements / Interactive Aurora UI
Where depth lives On the elements — shadow stack and lift In the background — drifting colour fields
Motion Pointer-driven, only while hovering Ambient, runs without input
Card treatment 1px #7f9bff border, blue drop shadow backdrop blur 14px, 1px white at 18%
Headline ● Extruded via multi-layer text-shadow Flat, sits on a scrim
Cost Transform per card; cheap but needs throttling Three composited layers; needs a pause control
Touch behaviour Must be disabled — no hover on a phone ● Works as-is; nothing depends on a pointer

When each one wins

3D Elements / Interactive wins when

  • The page is a product or a demo where the interaction itself is the argument
  • There are few cards and the user will actually point at them
  • You can gate the effect behind a hover media query so phones get the flat state

Aurora UI wins when

  • The page is a hero or a landing section where atmosphere matters more than interaction
  • You want the site to feel alive with no input at all — a kiosk, a launch page, a title screen
  • You need the effect to work identically on touch

Steering a model to one or the other

Split the instruction between object and air. For 3D, say the depth is a multi-layer text-shadow with 1px steps and that cards tilt on hover toward the pointer, with perspective on the parent. For aurora, say the depth is three radial gradients at 50% radius drifting on different durations behind static glass panels. A model asked for "dark 3D glassmorphism" will do both at once — moving fields under cards that also have drop shadows and tilt — and three depth cues will fight each other.
