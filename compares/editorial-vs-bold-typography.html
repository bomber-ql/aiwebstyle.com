Editorial vs Bold Typography

Both are pages where type is the entire design. One controls how far a line may run; the other controls how large a word may get.

Maintained by AI Web Style·Updated 28 October 2026

Short answer: Editorial caps the measure — 17 characters for the headline, 46 for the standfirst. Bold typography caps nothing and pushes size instead: clamp(3rem, 10.5vw, 7.2rem) at line-height 0.86, in pure black on pure white.

What actually separates them

Editorial is a reading style. The headline is 17ch and the standfirst 46ch, a serif display face over a sans body, and the sections are divided by 1px rules rather than cards. Nothing about it is loud; the whole design is the argument that a line of text has a correct length.

Bold typography is a display style. The headline goes to 7.2rem on wide screens with line-height 0.86, letter-spacing tight enough that words nearly touch, and a 12ch measure that forces three or four lines out of a short sentence. There is no second colour, no image, no card: the page is black text on white, and the only variable is scale.

Decision table

A filled dot marks the side that handles the dimension more convincingly. It is a judgement, not a rule — several rows are genuinely a tie.

Dimension Editorial / Magazine Bold Typography
Headline size clamp(2.5rem, 6vw, 4.2rem) ● clamp(3rem, 10.5vw, 7.2rem)
Headline measure 17ch ● 12ch
Line height 1.02 ● 0.86
Typeface Serif display, sans body Helvetica Neue only
Palette Paper #fbfaf7, ink #14130f, one rust accent Pure #ffffff and #0a0a0a
Separation 1px rules between sections 2px rules and large white gaps

The CSS that changes

These are the declarations that make the difference visible. Everything else — font sizes, spacing, section order — is shared, because both pages use the same markup.

Property Editorial / Magazine Bold Typography
Headline font-size: clamp(2.5rem, 6vw, 4.2rem); max-width: 17ch font-size: clamp(3rem, 10.5vw, 7.2rem); max-width: 12ch
Leading line-height: 1.02 line-height: .86
Standfirst 1.16rem, max-width: 46ch 1.04rem, max-width: 40ch
Font stack Playfair Display headings, Lato body 'Helvetica Neue', Helvetica, Arial

When each one wins

Editorial / Magazine wins when

  • The page is read in sequence and the reader should never lose their place
  • There is a deck or standfirst — a real one, not a subtitle repeating the headline
  • The content is long enough that fatigue is the enemy, not attention

Bold Typography wins when

  • The page has one sentence to deliver and no body copy worth reading
  • The brand is confident enough to spend an entire screen on four words
  • You want scale to do the work that imagery would normally do

Steering a model to one or the other

Give the model a measure for editorial (17ch, 46ch) and a scale for bold typography (clamp up to 7rem, line-height under 0.9). Asked for "big bold type" without a line-height, a model will keep 1.5 and the block will look like a poster with the air let out of it. Asked for "editorial" without a measure, you get the browser default of roughly 140 characters — twice what a reader can track.
