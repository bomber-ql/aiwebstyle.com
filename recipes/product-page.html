Product Detail Page

A product page answers one question: is this the right thing, and what happens if it is not. Everything else is decoration. The buy box carries the first half, the trust strip carries the second, and the description exists to absorb the doubts those two could not.

Maintained by AI Web Style·Updated 28 October 2026

Commerce 8 sections 6 variables 8 min read

What this page has to do

Commerce pages fail on mobile first. The add-to-cart button lands below a full-height image gallery, the variant selector changes the price but not the photograph, and the delivery estimate says 3 to 5 business days without ever naming a date.

There is a second failure that matters more for a real store: invented social proof. A model asked for reviews will produce a 4.8 average from 2,341 reviews, which is both false and obvious. The prompt below requires an honest placeholder instead, and separates the parts of the page a developer must wire up from the parts that are static.

Section order

The order is the argument. Reordering these is not a layout decision, it is a change to the reasoning.

  1. 01

    Breadcrumb

    Three levels maximum, current page not a link. It tells the shopper where they are without costing a row of vertical space.

  2. 02

    Gallery

    Four images minimum, including one in use and one that shows scale. Never auto-advance — a carousel that moves while someone is reading is a carousel they will misread.

  3. 03

    Buy box

    Name, price with tax basis, variant, quantity, add to cart, delivery date. All of it above the fold on a phone; this is the whole page in one column.

  4. 04

    Trust strip

    Four items maximum. Shipping cost, return window, warranty, payment methods — each answerable in one sentence, all of them things the shopper would otherwise email about.

  5. 05

    Description

    Three paragraphs: what it is, what it is made of, what it is not for. The last one prevents the return.

  6. 06

    Specification table

    Six to ten rows with units in the header. This is the section that gets copied into a purchasing spreadsheet.

  7. 07

    Reviews

    Summary with a real count, then three reviews with dates. Show the three-star review first; a page of unanimous praise reads as manufactured.

  8. 08

    Cross-sell

    Three related items with visible prices. Anything that requires a click to learn the price will not be clicked.

Build the prompt

6 variables. Change one and the text below rewrites itself — nothing else on this page moves.

Assembled prompt 
Build a product detail page for one physical product in the apparel. The variant control is size and colour, the size guide is a link inside the buy box, the return window is 30 days and the model must show a garment on a body and flat. category.

AUDIENCE
A shopper who arrived from a search for this exact product and is choosing between it and one alternative. They are on a phone, on mobile data, and they will leave if the price or the add-to-cart button is not visible early.

STRUCTURE (exactly this order)
1. Breadcrumb: three levels maximum. The current page is text, not a link.
2. Gallery: Show the gallery as a sticky left column of stacked images; the buy box scrolls independently on the right. On mobile, collapse to a horizontal swipe strip with visible dots and no autoplay. At least four images: one in use, one showing scale against a familiar object, one detail, one packaging. Never auto-advance.
3. Buy box, beside the gallery on desktop and directly beneath it on mobile: product name as h1; price with currency symbol and a tax note; size as five rectangular buttons with the sold-out one struck through, colour as circular swatches with a text label that updates; both selection states visible without hover. quantity stepper; add to cart as the only filled button on the page; delivery estimate as a date range, not a number of days.
4. Trust strip: Four items: free shipping over a stated threshold with a delivery date range; 30-day returns with who pays postage; two-year warranty; accepted payment methods as text, not logos. Four items maximum, one sentence each.
5. Description: three paragraphs. What it is, what it is made of, what it is not for.
6. Specification table: 6 to 10 rows, units in the column header, no marketing language.
7. Reviews: a rating summary with a real count, then three reviews with dates and a verified badge. Include one review below four stars.
8. Cross-sell: three related products using the same card shape, with prices visible and no hover-only information.

STYLE
Clean commerce: white surface, one neutral grey scale, a single accent reserved for price and the add-to-cart button, 1px hairlines instead of cards, generous 1.25rem line height, product photography with no decorative frame.

MOTION
No animation beyond the native feel of a button press. Commerce pages lose money to motion that delays the buy box.

COPY RULES
- Price always carries a currency symbol and states whether tax is included.
- Every measurement carries a unit. No size called large without a number beside it.
- Banned: premium quality, must-have, game-changer, elevate, curated for you, crafted with care.
- Shipping cost, delivery window and the return period must all be answerable from this page without opening another.
- Reviews must be labelled as placeholder content, with the comment Review data to be supplied by the store. Do not invent an average rating or a review count.

CONSTRAINTS
- Add to cart fully visible at 390x844 without scrolling.
- Changing a variant must update price and image together, never one alone.
- All tap targets at least 44x44 CSS pixels, including the quantity stepper.
- Every image needs width and height attributes so nothing shifts while loading.
- No countdown timers, no stock counters, no exit-intent modal, no chat bubble over the buy box.

OUTPUT
One page plus an inert cart drawer in the markup. Static where possible; mark the four places that need real store data with a comment.

What to change, and what to leave alone

VariableHow to edit it
{{CATEGORY}} This rewrites what the page has to prove. Apparel proves fit, electronics proves specification, food proves provenance. Keeping the copy and swapping the category produces a page that answers the wrong doubt.
{{GALLERY}} Sticky columns work when the buy box is tall. Four-up grids work when the product is visually distinctive. Carousels are the fallback, not the default.
{{VARIANTS}} If you have variants, the price must be per variant, not starting at. Starting-at pricing on a product page is the single most common cause of cart abandonment.
{{TRUST}} Only claim what you can honour. A 30-day return window you do not offer will generate chargebacks that cost more than the sale.
{{STYLE}} Photography decides this more than taste does. Dark styling on white-background product shots looks like a rendering error, not a premium choice.

Feeding it to each builder

BuilderWhat to do differently
v0 Generate the buy box alone first. It is the only section with real logic, and generating it inside the full page makes iteration slow because every change re-renders the gallery.
Lovable It will invent reviews and a review count unless the placeholder rule is present. Check the rating summary before you publish; that is the line most likely to be quietly fabricated.
Bolt Ask for static HTML and CSS with one small script for variant state. A framework build here adds a dependency for what is fundamentally a form.
Cursor / Claude Code Point it at your product data file first and tell it to read prices and variants from there. Hard-coded prices in markup are the defect that survives longest.

Where it usually breaks

01The buy box below the gallery on mobile

A full-height image followed by a full-height gallery pushes the price and the button past the first screen. On a phone the buy box comes first and the gallery second, or half the visitors never learn the price.

02Variant changes that only update the price

Selecting a different colour and seeing the same photograph is a trust failure, not a polish issue. The shopper concludes the images are stock and the variants may not exist.

03Delivery described in days

Three to five business days requires the shopper to do calendar arithmetic, and they will do it pessimistically. A date range does the work for them and converts better at identical cost.

04Invented reviews

A 4.8 average from 2,341 reviews on a page that launched last week is both false and detectable. It is also the fastest route to a marketplace suspension if this is a real store.
