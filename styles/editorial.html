About This Style

Editorial layout is print typography translated without apology: a serif display face, a measured column, a clear ladder from kicker to headline to standfirst to body. It reads as credible because the hierarchy is borrowed from publications that have refined it for a century.

The screen-specific problem is width. Print measures around 60–70 characters per line; a browser will happily give you 140. Fixing that single constraint — a max-width on the body column — does more for the style than any typeface choice.

measure: 62ch ladder: 5 steps display: serif rules: 1px hairline no cards accent: 1 colour

Main Features:

  • A real type ladder: Kicker, headline, standfirst, body, caption. Five distinct roles, each with its own size, weight and colour.
  • Measured line length: 62–68 characters for body copy. Anything wider and the eye loses the return sweep.
  • Serif display, sans body: The classic pairing. Serif for voice, sans for extended reading — or serif throughout if the face is well hinted.
  • Asymmetric columns: A wide body column beside a narrow note column, not a symmetrical 50/50 split.
  • Rules instead of boxes: Hairline rules and whitespace separate content. Cards and shadows are what magazines do not use.

Example AI Prompt

Design an editorial page layout.

Typography:
- Display face: a serif with a strong contrast axis, used for the headline only. Body: a neutral sans or a text serif at 17-19px with line-height 1.6.
- Build a five-step ladder: kicker (11-12px, letter-spaced uppercase), headline (clamp 2.4rem to 4rem, tight leading), standfirst (1.15rem, lighter colour), body, caption (0.8rem, muted).

Measure:
- Body column max-width 62ch. This is the single most important constraint — do not let text run wider.
- Use a two-column asymmetric grid for sections: 8 columns of copy, 4 of notes.

Styling:
- No cards, no shadows, no rounded corners. Separate blocks with 1px hairlines and whitespace.
- One accent colour used sparingly — a rule, a kicker, a link underline.

Constraints:
- Do not justify text; ragged-right is correct for the screen.
- Keep paragraph spacing at 1em and drop any indent — indents plus spacing reads as a mistake.
- Headline leading must be below 1.1; anything looser loses the print feel.

Where it fits

Use it when

  • Long-form articles, essays, documentation with a voice, or a company blog that wants authority.
  • Product launches that lean on narrative rather than feature grids.
  • Any page where reading is the primary action.

Avoid it when

  • Dense application UI. A serif ladder in a dashboard slows scanning.
  • Pages where users compare items — editorial layout has no table grammar.
  • If you cannot source a good display serif. A weak serif is worse than a neutral sans.
